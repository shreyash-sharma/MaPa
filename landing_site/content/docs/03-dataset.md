# Data behind the model

Real phone logs, scaled up. MaPa learns from real phone-activity logs and from parent timelines generated by a statistical model fitted to those logs: 200 parent profiles of 120 days each, and more than 4 million training rows. To stress-test the scoring policy under controlled conditions, labeled behavior cases are programmatically added to the timelines; they are not naturally observed events or emergency records.

| Data group | Use and origin |
|---|---|
| S3 and UbiqLog phone logs | Source records used to build observed activity bins and fit the timeline generator. See the source datasets for their own terms and attribution requirements. |
| Model-derived parent timelines | 200 parent profiles over 120 days each, generated from a model fitted to phone logs. Exact-copy checks were part of the dataset work. |
| Person-level development split | Train: 162 people; validation: 39; historical test: 39. |
| Separate real-log check group | 11 people held out from fitting; results are descriptive and do not provide labeled event-detection evidence. |

The published dataset description reports 4,230,516 train rows, 495,523 validation rows, and 492,687 historical test rows. It also reports 532 real training days among roughly 44,000 training person-days; real 15-minute steps make up about 0.09% of training rows.

## How the data is built

1. **Real logs become quarter-hours.** Phone-activity records from the S3 and UbiqLog studies are turned into the same 15-minute bins the phone produces: used, not used, or not observed. Only strictly observed windows count as observed; gaps are never filled in with assumed activity.
2. **A generator learns their rhythm.** A statistical model is fitted to those real bins: phone sessions, quiet-stretch lengths (handled correctly when a stretch runs past the end of a log) and how much people differ from one another, with an age adjustment based on published statistics. Empty observation is never read as low use, and the fitting step is technically blocked from reading held-out people.
3. **Timelines at scale.** The generator draws 200 new parent timelines of 120 days each, with exact-copy and privacy checks against the real logs.
4. **Labeled behavior cases.** Ten scenario types are programmatically added on top, including positive cases graded mild, clear and severe, and expected-none cases that *look* quiet but should not alert: a delayed morning, a tagged day, a normal long silence, daytime inactivity and a phone left at home. The frozen release holds 30,256 labeled episodes.

## Built for honest evaluation

- **People never cross splits.** Train, validation and test are split by person, so the model is always scored on people it has never seen.
- **Frozen and hash-locked.** The release is immutable: every file is listed with its SHA-256 hash, and the manifest is the hash root. Training reads the frozen split, so a result can always be traced to the exact bytes behind it.
- **A real-log group nobody touched.** The 11 real-log check people are kept out of fitting and out of the release entirely.
- **Fresh cohorts for each claim.** The v2 numbers in `04-results.md` come from a new cohort generated *after* the policy was locked, from a fresh seed, with no fitting or tuning on it.

## Rights and limits

The real logs do not represent the full age range or routine diversity of intended users; widening them is part of the pilot. Source terms, attribution and redistribution permissions are checked per dataset before any data is published; no single blanket license covers every source and derived output. The web demo uses fictional example records only, and no real person's timeline is embedded in it.
