## Uses

**Intended use.** Inside the MaPa app, on the parent's own phone: estimate when the phone is usually used, and flag a day that looks very
different from this person's own rhythm, so a family member can decide to call. Only the conclusion leaves the phone.

**Out of scope.** Not a medical device, not an emergency or fall detector, and not a safety product. Do not use it to judge health,
sleep, mood or safety, to monitor a person without their consent, or to make decisions about a person without a human in the loop.
There is no evaluation with real parents in a real emergency.

On Android, load `rhythm-step.pte` and `rhythm-daily.pte` with ExecuTorch. The app code that does this, with a Kotlin fallback that gives

## Risks and limitations (short)

- Test disruptions are injected, not observed emergencies.
- Real data is under 1% of training days, and the real people were mostly younger than the intended users.
- On normal days that only look quiet, it still alerted more often than wanted: on 139 of 368 injected normal-quiet episodes (37.8%).
- Inputs exclude movement, real battery and real charging in this version (the training data had placeholders for them).
- Built for families in India, but not validated on Indian older adults yet.
- The test split was opened once, after the selection was locked; there was no tuning on it.
- An early pilot is planned; no accuracy claim is made from it.

# MaPa Rhythm Hybrid

**Release status: the locked validation confirmation and one-time final test are complete. This is a research artifact, not a safety product.**

Trained on real smartphone logs, plus parent timelines generated by a statistical model learned from those logs. Every disruption is injected.

MaPa Rhythm Hybrid is a causal 15-minute rhythm model intended to estimate near-term phone use and score injected deviations from a person's usual phone-use pattern. It does not identify emergencies, illness, sleep, or a person's physical state. A disruption label in the benchmark is injected, not an observed emergency. No medical or safety claims are made.

## Architecture and inference

The current selected architecture has 9,594 parameters and uses 21 inputs per 15-minute step, including session starts. The fast lane is a causal dilated CNN followed by a GRU; attention was dropped after the train-only ablation. The slow lane is an LSTM over up to 30 daily-summary rows, with a personal context vector. Heads produce 16 next-use hazards, current-use, unusual-quiet and confidence outputs. The first next-use probability is calibrated as 0.15 times the learned forecast plus 0.85 times the profile rate; that weight was selected using train OOF next-bin log-loss only. Other forecast horizons are unchanged. The decision configuration selected on train OOF uses combined score, personal q=0.90, floor=0.50, morning check off, silence floor 32 bins, cold-start enabled, model weight 1.0 and uncertainty margin 0.1. Stale inputs must always be treated as monitoring unavailable by the calling application.

The exported files are `rhythm-step.pte` and `rhythm-daily.pte` for ExecuTorch/XNNPACK; `hybrid_reference.weights.txt` and the two `golden_hybrid_*.txt` files are for the Kotlin reference/parity path. `export-manifest.json` records SHA-256 values and runtimes. `load_example.py` checks the checkpoint and calibration hashes and runs one causal step.

## Training data and method

The frozen `final-v2` dataset has 162 train people and 4,230,516 train rows; validation has 39 people / 495,523 rows; test has 39 people / 492,687 rows. Train contains 1,624 injected behaviour episodes (781 mild, 502 clear, 341 severe). Only train was used for fitting and selection; validation and test were each opened once, after the selection was locked (see Locked held-out evaluation). The 11 `real_check` people were not used for fitting. Real observed training bins are derived from S3 and UbiqLog; see [source attribution](DATA-ATTRIBUTION.md) and the per-source licence texts ([S3](licenses/s3-CC-BY-4.0.txt), [UbiqLog](licenses/ubiqlog-CC-BY-4.0.txt)). No row-level source dataset is included in this model package. In the final fit, stage 1 used 3,828 observed real bins (3,772 next-use targets) across 532 real-person days from 22 train people; 1 real day passed the >=14-hour daily-summary rule. Partial days contributed masked step targets, not slow-lane rows or stage-2/person-limit history.

Training used 3 epochs of real observed-bin masked/next-use pretraining, 5 epochs on model-derived normal timelines and 7 epochs on injected behaviour episodes. Train-only day-swap used five nearest same-person days subject to the 10th-percentile distance filter. Source weights were applied as described in the protocol. Cold-start history prefixes were sampled from 0 to 30 days. The checkpoint was fitted on all train people after the selected architecture/configuration was locked by five-fold person-held-out OOF results.

## Train-only results (selection evidence, not a held-out claim)

Row 9, one seed across five person folds (33 / 33 / 32 / 32 / 32 people), selected 2.5857 false alerts per person-week, with behaviour catches of 99.21% mild (775/781), 98.86% clear (496/502), and 100.00% severe (341/341). Stale-as-fresh was 0. It passed the train minimum and C4 gates against the 4-hour and statistics-only baselines. The mean hard-negative alert rate was 64.89%, a substantial limitation. Observation catches are separate: 2,396/2,396 mild, 2,324/2,324 clear, and 2,276/2,276 severe.

### Next-use log-loss

On 3,772 untouched real train OOF bins from 22 people, the raw neural forecast scored 0.7909 [0.6939, 0.9296]; profile-only scored 0.6893 [0.5557, 0.8274]. The selected 0.15/0.85 model/profile blend scored 0.6857 [0.5722, 0.8247]. Person-bootstrap intervals overlap substantially; evidence of a real improvement is uncertain. The blend was selected using next-use loss only. The fixed row-9 detector audit with this post-processing had unchanged FA/week, behaviour catches, C4 and stale results.

## Export and parity

The trained checkpoint has 9,594 parameters. ExecuTorch 0.7.0/XNNPACK exports are 103,684 bytes (step) and 123,136 bytes (daily). Runtime parity passed 192 streaming steps and daily-history counts 0, 1, 3, 14 and 30: max absolute errors 2.384e-6 (stream) and 1.788e-7 (daily), tolerance 1e-3. Kotlin trained-weight parity passed 192 streaming rows (max error 9.537e-7) and five daily-history boundaries (8.941e-8). These checks establish numerical parity for fixtures, not Android app integration, field performance, or phone latency. The Parent app ModelStore still loads the earlier CNN; this Hybrid export is not yet wired into the running app.

## Limitations and pending checks

- Validation and test were each opened once, after the selection was locked; results are in Locked held-out evaluation below.
- The Parent Android app is not yet running this Hybrid: its `ModelStore` currently loads the earlier CNN, and there is no HybridReference/Hybrid checkpoint integration call site in the app. Pixel 6a latency is unmeasured and no device is attached.
- The selected full system has a 64.89% hard-negative alert rate; the model-alone diagnostic is 50.55%, still high. Train OOF results do not establish acceptable performance in families' use.
- The cold-start first-week catch rate is unmeasured because that band contains no behaviour episodes; the 8-14-day sample is small.
- There are only 22 real people in final-v2 train and few real observed bins; most training timelines are model-derived. Derived-to-real transfer is uncertain.
- Forecast blend and detector results are selected/evaluated on train OOF; confidence intervals overlap and independent validation is required.
- No location, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, voice, or raw app names are model inputs. Session starts are a use-burst proxy, not device unlocks. Movement is unknown in final-v2 and is not claimed as learned.
- No medical, emergency detection, or safety claims.

## Prior work we learned from

Ideas adopted, no code or data reused. None of these is the paper of this model; MaPa has no paper yet.

- Shen et al. (2025), IEEE BHI 2025 (preprint number 2509.23158): day-swap augmentation, similarity weighting, 14-hour daily-summary coverage gate, and sequence modeling adapted to 15-minute steps.
- Cejudo et al. (2025), CASAS routine prediction: next-use forecasting, per-person sensitivity and deviation explanations.
- Gibietz et al. (2026), Gerontechnology / CASAS HH101: learn normal patterns and adapt a per-person threshold using recent normal history.
- ElderWatch AI and eNeighbor informed alert-level and morning-check experiments; the morning-check setting was not selected. StillFine's parent-first interaction remains a product idea and was not evaluated.

These are design inspirations, not reproductions of their datasets or reported performance. The implementation-to-reference mapping is maintained in the project training specification and research notes.

## Reproduce

Training and evaluation code: `code/` here and `mapa-android/routine-engine/ml/mapa_train/` in the GitHub repository.
Seeds: 20261101 to 20261105 (the final fit uses 20261101). Data: [MaPa-Rhythm](https://huggingface.co/datasets/shreyashsharma/MaPa-Rhythm), `final-v2`.

```bash
python -m mapa_train.parity --features-only        # Python features match the phone
python -m mapa_train.final_eval --split test --render-saved   # re-renders the saved one-time test table
```

The Android parity checks (Kotlin and ExecuTorch against PyTorch, and the full on-phone pipeline against the tested Python pipeline)
run with `./gradlew :routine-engine:test` in the GitHub repository. File hashes: `hf-dry-run-manifest.json`.
