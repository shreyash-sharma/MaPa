## 1. Submission text

**Name:** MaPa

**One line:** Know their day began as usual, without ever asking.
(Alternative, safer: "Know when their day looks different, without ever asking." The first is used only where the screen never shows a good state on old data.)

**What it is.** MaPa gives Indian families a calm daily picture of an aging parent's day from the parent's own
phone use. The parent's phone learns that parent's rhythm, and the family sees when a day looks very different
from it, the same day. The family does the setup; the parent taps Yes and allows two permissions (usage access and physical activity). Only conclusions leave the parent's
phone: never app names, messages, location, audio or screen contents. No GPS, no camera, no medical claims.

**The model (ours, trained from scratch).** The MaPa Rhythm Hybrid: a causal 1D CNN and a GRU over 15-minute
steps, a 30-day LSTM over daily summaries, a personal context from the parent's own history, a starting profile so it starts from day one, and a decision layer with an alert line for each parent and an 8-hour safety floor.
9,594 parameters, 21 inputs. Trained on real smartphone logs, plus parent timelines generated by a statistical
model learned from those logs. Every disruption is injected.

**Result (held-out test, 39 people, opened once).** MaPa caught all 93 injected disruptions in the held-out
test (40/40 mild, 31/31 clear, 22/22 severe), with 2.4 false alerts per person-week. For comparison on the same
test: a 4-hour rule raised 6.1 false alerts per person-week and caught 20/40 injected mild disruptions; an
8-hour rule had the same false-alert rate as MaPa (2.4) but caught 5/40 injected mild and 10/31 injected clear
disruptions; a statistics-only baseline raised 3.6 and caught 7/40 injected mild disruptions.

**How we use Meta.**
- **ExecuTorch** runs the model on the parent's phone (XNNPACK): two programs, 103,684 and 123,136 bytes;
  0.36 ms per 15-minute step on a Pixel 6a (median of 1,000 steps, instrumented test). Parity with PyTorch: max
  error 2.4e-6.
- **Meta's Muse Spark open model, served on Baseten**, in the family app only: the daily note in English and Hindi,
  "why was she quiet?" in the family's own words to a life tag, and Ask MaPa. It receives a name-free summary,
  never raw activity. The parent's phone never calls Meta or any AI API. Questions about health or safety are
  answered on the phone with a fixed reply and never sent.

**What makes it hold up.** Illness guards (a new day waits before it is learned; life tags such as unwell,
travelling, guests or festival are skipped; a changed routine becomes normal only after a person says yes);
old data always shows as "Monitoring unavailable", never as a good day; starts from day one with a starting profile; the parent's phone
reads up to a week of past phone use at setup.

**Limits (stated plainly).**
- Test disruptions are injected, not observed emergencies.
- About 1% of the training days are real (532 of about 44,000 person-days); real 15-minute steps are 0.09% of training rows.
- The real people in the data were mostly younger than our users.
- The tested "please call" line is cautious: on a day that goes quiet after 07:45 it alerted at about
  14:45 to 15:15 (measured on a golden parent), shortly before the 8-hour floor.
- On normal days that only look quiet, MaPa still alerted more often than we want (the weakest part of the test):
  MaPa alerted inside 231 of 368 injected normal-quiet episodes (62.8%); the 8-hour rule alone alerted in 139 of 368 (37.8%).
- An early pilot with our own parents is planned; we make no accuracy claim from it.
